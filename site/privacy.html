Privacy statement

Effective November 25, 2026 · Version 2026-11-25 · What changed

This statement applies to the SentySurf app (version 2.23.1 and later) on Windows, macOS and Linux, and to the website sentysurf.com. It replaces the statement dated 2026-11-23; section 13 lists what changed. The same statement is in the app under Privacy.

At a glance

  • SentySurf is a security monitor that runs on your computer. Its checks run on the computer and their results stay there.
  • There is no account, no advertising, no analytics and no usage tracking. You never have to give us personal information to use SentySurf. We do not sell or share your personal information, and we never see the contents of your files, emails, messages or browsing.
  • A few features use the internet: update checks (GitHub), the password check if you use it (Have I Been Pwned), app update lists on Windows (Microsoft winget) and, if you set it up, family mode. Family summaries are encrypted on your computer before they are sent, so the relays cannot read them.
  • Everything SentySurf keeps is in one folder on your computer that you can open, clear or delete. Optional features can be turned off at any time.
  • Questions and requests: support@sentysurf.com.

1. Who we are and how to contact us

SentySurf ("SentySurf", "we", "us") is a free security monitor developed and operated from Alberta, Canada. For the personal information described here that reaches us (family-mode relay messages, website requests and emails you send us), SentySurf is the organization responsible for it, called the "controller" under the EU and UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

Privacy questions, requests and complaints: support@sentysurf.com. Messages to this address reach the person accountable for privacy at SentySurf.

2. What SentySurf processes on your computer

To check your computer's security, SentySurf reads the information below. It is processed on your computer only. We do not receive it, except for the limited items listed in section 4.

What it reads

  • Security settings: firewall, antivirus, encryption, updates, sharing and sign-in options.
  • Programs and system: running programs, their network connections and digital signatures; startup entries, scheduled tasks and services; installed programs and their versions; members of the Administrators group.
  • Event logs: Windows event logs for failed sign-ins and blocked apps.
  • Network: your Wi-Fi network's name and security type (never its password); the hosts file and network settings such as proxy and DNS; the connection records that remote-control apps (AnyDesk, TeamViewer, RustDesk) and Windows Remote Desktop keep; the devices your computer already sees on your home network.
  • Browsers: installed browsers and their version numbers, extensions and browser settings (home page, search engine, start-up pages, policies).
  • Camera and microphone: the system's own record of which apps used them. SentySurf never records or receives audio or video.
  • Ransomware shield: its own tripwire files, and the names (not the contents) of files at the top of your main folders, to spot ransom notes.
  • Clipboard Guard: copied text, in memory only, to see whether it is a crypto wallet address. If it catches a swap, the alert shows a shortened form of the two addresses (first 6 and last 4 characters). Nothing else you copy is saved.
  • Safety check: files and links, only when you choose them.

SentySurf never reads the contents of your documents, emails or messages, your browsing history, cookies or saved passwords, and it does not collect your location.

Do you have to provide anything?

No. No law or contract requires you to give us personal information, and SentySurf works without an account. A few optional features need something from you to work: the password check needs the password you type, family mode needs a family code, and SIEM forwarding needs the destination you choose. If you don't provide it, only that feature is unavailable.

What it keeps, and where

SentySurf keeps its baseline (a snapshot of your system's known-good state), scan results and score history, alert history, settings and allowlist, the home network device list (addresses, hardware addresses and names you give devices), the apps it has seen go online, fingerprints of program files it has already signature-checked and, if you use them, your family code and SIEM settings.

It is all in one folder. With administrator rights (the normal case on Windows) that is C:\ProgramData\SentySurf, which only administrators and the system can open. Without them it is ~/Library/Application Support/SentySurf on a Mac and ~/.sentysurf on Linux, readable only by your account. A SIEM token is encrypted with Windows DPAPI on Windows. The family code is stored as it is, protected by the folder's permissions. Reports you choose to save are stored where you save them.

3. Changes SentySurf makes to your computer

SentySurf only changes, removes or ends something when you click a button that clearly says so, such as Fix it, Update all, Remove or End it, or when you turn on live protection, which registers SentySurf to start when you sign in. There are two exceptions:

  • Automatic updates (on by default; Settings > Install updates automatically): verified new versions of SentySurf are installed.
  • Ransomware shield (on by default; Live protection > Ransomware shield): two small hidden tripwire documents are placed in each of your main folders (Documents, Desktop, Pictures, Videos, Music, Downloads and any you add). If it sees files being encrypted, it pauses the app most likely doing it, which you can resume or end in one click. Tripwires never leave your computer, but if those folders are synced (for example by OneDrive or iCloud), the tripwires are synced with them like any other file. Turning the shield off removes them, and so does uninstalling on Windows. On a Mac or Linux, turn the shield off before removing the app.

Family summaries contain the device name, SentySurf version, operating system and version, maker and model, processor type, memory, disk size and free space, when the computer was last started and last scanned, score, issue counts, the number of apps with updates, whether protection is on or paused, and the titles of high and medium alerts from the last 14 days that you have not marked as reviewed. For remote-control alerts they also include what SentySurf found about the session: which app, the connecting person's app ID or name, the network addresses involved, and when; for Windows Remote Desktop, the account the session signed in to and the connecting computer's name. They never include files, browsing, camera or microphone activity, or serial numbers, and no other account names. Each summary is encrypted on your computer with a key made from your family code (Fernet: AES-128 with HMAC-SHA256; key derived with PBKDF2, 200,000 rounds) and sent on a channel name derived from the same code. Nobody without the code can read it, including us. If you also set a shared folder, the same encrypted summary is written there every 5 minutes.

Those session details can identify the person who connected, who did not give them to us. SentySurf takes them from the records the remote-control app or Windows already keeps on your computer, so that you, and your family if you use family mode, can see who connected. They stay on your computer and in the encrypted family summary, which we cannot read.

Safety check rates links on your computer and never opens, looks up or sends them. Files are checked on your computer: on Windows by Microsoft Defender when it is your active antivirus, on a Mac by Gatekeeper. Defender and Gatekeeper may consult Microsoft or Apple under their own settings.

5. Why we process it: purposes and legal bases

We use personal information only for the purposes below. In Canada we rely on your consent, which you give by installing SentySurf and turning on optional features, and which you can withdraw by turning a feature off or uninstalling. Under the GDPR and UK GDPR, our legal bases are:

  • Providing features you ask for (password check, family mode, app update lists, home network checks, SIEM forwarding): necessary to provide the service you requested (Article 6(1)(b)).
  • Keeping SentySurf up to date and secure (update checks and verified automatic updates): our legitimate interest, and yours, in security software that stays current (Article 6(1)(f)). Automatic installs can be turned off in Settings.
  • Delivering the website and the family relay and protecting them from abuse: our legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Answering emails you send us: to act on your request (Article 6(1)(b)) and our legitimate interest in replying (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Meeting legal obligations, where they apply (Article 6(1)(c)).

You can object to processing based on legitimate interests by emailing us. We do not use personal information for advertising or profiling, and we make no automated decisions that have legal or similarly significant effects on you. The security score is worked out on your computer, for your information only.

6. Service providers and other recipients

  • Cloudflare, Inc. (United States): runs sentysurf.com and relay.sentysurf.com, and processes each request, including your IP address, to deliver and protect them. Cloudflare acts as our service provider (processor).
  • GitHub, Inc. (United States): hosts SentySurf's releases, update information and downloads. GitHub is an independent controller under its own privacy statement.
  • ntfy.sh: a public notification service used as the second family relay. It receives only encrypted messages and operates under its own policy.
  • Have I Been Pwned: receives the 5-character hash prefix when you use the password check, under its own policy.
  • Microsoft: winget's app catalogue on Windows, and Microsoft Defender if it is your antivirus, under Microsoft's privacy statement. Apple: Gatekeeper on macOS, under Apple's settings and policy.
  • Destinations you choose: your SIEM servers, and websites you open from the app.

Cloudflare processes information for us under its data processing terms, which limit its use to providing its service and require it to protect the information. GitHub, ntfy.sh, Have I Been Pwned, Microsoft and Apple are independent services with their own privacy statements; SentySurf sends them no more than section 4 describes. We do not obtain personal information about you from data brokers or any other source.

We do not sell, rent or trade personal information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. We would disclose information only where the law requires it; the relay holds only encrypted data that we cannot read.

7. International transfers

SentySurf is operated from Canada. Cloudflare and GitHub are based in the United States and process data in many countries; Cloudflare's network answers from a location near you. Your information may therefore be processed outside your province or country, including in the United States, where it may be accessible to courts and authorities under local law. For information that leaves the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, we rely on Cloudflare's data processing terms, which include the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses. Family summaries are encrypted before they leave your computer, wherever they are stored. You can ask us about our service providers outside Canada at support@sentysurf.com.

8. How long information is kept

  • On your computer: until you delete it. Alert history is kept until you clear it (Privacy page > Clear alert history). Score history keeps the last 200 scans. A SIEM log file is kept up to about 20 MB, plus one older copy. Leaving a family (Family > Leave family) deletes this computer's family settings. On Windows the uninstaller offers to delete the whole data folder; on a Mac or Linux, delete the folder in section 2 after removing the app.
  • relay.sentysurf.com: encrypted family messages are purged after 12 hours. IP addresses are not stored in the relay's database, and SentySurf does not log or use them.
  • ntfy.sh, GitHub, Have I Been Pwned and Microsoft: under their own policies.
  • Emails you send us: as long as needed to help you and handle any follow-up, then deleted.

9. How we protect it

  • Processing stays on your computer wherever possible, and the data folder is readable only by administrators or by your account.
  • Family summaries are end-to-end encrypted. The relay accepts only SentySurf's encrypted message format, limits message size and frequency, and purges messages after 12 hours.
  • Connections use HTTPS with certificate checking. Updates are installed only if they match the published SHA-256 checksum.
  • On Windows, SIEM tokens are encrypted with DPAPI.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affecting your personal information occurs, we will notify you and the authorities as the law requires.

10. Your rights and choices

Your choices in the app: turn off optional features (family mode, Network watch, SIEM forwarding, automatic updates, ransomware shield), clear alert history, open or delete the data folder, leave a family, or uninstall SentySurf.

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to: know what personal information we hold about you and get a copy; have it corrected; have it deleted; restrict or object to its use; receive it in a portable format; withdraw consent; and not be discriminated against for using these rights. In Quebec you may also ask that information about you be de-indexed. Residents of Quebec, the EU, the UK and California have these rights under their laws; residents of other provinces and states have similar rights in many cases, and we honour requests from everyone.

To make a request, email support@sentysurf.com. It is free. We may ask you to confirm your identity, and you may use an authorized agent. We respond within 30 days, or tell you if we need more time where the law allows. Most information never reaches us: what SentySurf keeps is on your computer, where you can see and delete it yourself, and relay messages are encrypted, cannot be linked to you without your family code and are gone within 12 hours.

You can also complain to a privacy regulator, although we would welcome the chance to help first:

  • Canada: Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (priv.gc.ca)
  • Alberta: Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner of Alberta (oipc.ab.ca)
  • Quebec: Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec (cai.gouv.qc.ca)
  • European Union: the data protection authority in your country
  • United Kingdom: Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk)
  • California: California Privacy Protection Agency (cppa.ca.gov)

California notice at collection: in the last 12 months, the only personal information we have collected is identifiers (IP addresses, processed by Cloudflare for the website and relay), encrypted family summaries that we cannot read, and the contents of emails you send us. They come from you and your device, are used only for the purposes in section 5, are kept as described in section 8, and are not sold or shared. We do not use sensitive personal information to infer anything about you.

11. Children

SentySurf is not directed at children under 13, or under the age of digital consent where you live, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. A parent or guardian may set up family mode on a computer a child uses. The summary describes the computer's security, not the child, but it does include the device name, so choose a name that does not include a child's full name. If you think a child has sent us personal information, contact us and we will delete it.

12. The website and browser check

sentysurf.com sets no cookies and uses no analytics, advertising or tracking scripts; fonts and images are served from the site itself. The site is delivered through Cloudflare, which processes each request, including your IP address, to deliver and protect the site. Download buttons take you to the release files on GitHub, where GitHub's privacy statement applies.

The browser check (sentysurf.com/check) runs entirely in your browser. It reads details any website can read (browser version, language, screen size and so on) and shows them to you; it does not send them anywhere, store them or set cookies. The only request it makes is for a small file listing current browser versions.
