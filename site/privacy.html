Privacy

Last updated November 22, 2026 · applies to SentySurf 2.20 and later and to this website

SentySurf runs on your computer. It has no account, no advertising, no analytics and no usage tracking, and it doesn't open any network ports for others to connect to. This page lists everything it reads, keeps, changes and sends. The same statement is in the app under Privacy.

What SentySurf reads, on your computer only

  • Security settings: firewall, antivirus, encryption, updates, sharing and sign-in options.
  • Running programs, their network connections and digital signatures; startup entries, scheduled tasks and services; members of the Administrators group; installed programs and their versions.
  • Windows event logs for failed sign-ins and blocked apps; your Wi-Fi network's name and security type (never its password); the hosts file and network settings such as proxy and DNS.
  • Browser extensions and browser settings (home page, search engine, start-up pages, policies).
  • The system's own record of which apps used the camera or microphone, and the devices your computer already sees on your home network.
  • The ransomware shield reads its own tripwire files and the names (not contents) of files at the top of your main folders, to spot ransom notes.
  • Clipboard Guard checks copied text in memory only to see whether it's a crypto wallet address. If it catches a swap, the alert shows a shortened form of the two addresses; nothing else you copy is saved.
  • Files and links only when you choose them on the Safety check page.

SentySurf never reads the contents of your documents, emails or messages, your browsing history, cookies or saved passwords.

What it keeps, and where

Its baseline (a snapshot of your system's known-good state), scan results and score history, alert history, settings and allowlist, the home network device list (addresses, hardware addresses and names you give devices), the apps it has seen go online, fingerprints of program files it has already signature-checked, and, if you use them, your family code and SIEM settings.

It's all in one folder. With administrator rights (the normal case on Windows) that's C:\ProgramData\SentySurf, which only administrators and the system can open. Without them it's ~/Library/Application Support/SentySurf on a Mac and ~/.sentysurf on Linux, readable only by your account. A SIEM token is encrypted with Windows DPAPI on Windows. The family code is stored as it is, protected by the folder's permissions. You can clear alert history on the app's Privacy page. On Windows the uninstaller offers to delete the whole folder; on a Mac or Linux, delete it after removing the app.

What it sends over the network

  • SentySurf updates. About every 6 hours while live protection or the window is running, SentySurf asks GitHub (api.github.com), where its releases are published, for the latest version. Like any web request, GitHub sees your IP address and SentySurf's version number; nothing else about you or your computer is sent. With automatic updates on, a new version is downloaded from GitHub over HTTPS and installed only if it matches the release's published SHA-256 checksum.
  • Password check (only when you use it). The first 5 characters of the password's SHA-1 hash go to Have I Been Pwned (api.pwnedpasswords.com), which answers with matching hashes padded with decoys. Your password never leaves your computer and is never saved.
  • App update list (Windows, during scans, including the daily automatic scan). Windows' built-in winget tool asks Microsoft's app catalogue which of your apps have updates. On macOS and Linux, SentySurf reads the update lists the system already has and makes no connection of its own.
  • Home network. Scan network, when you click it, sends one empty packet to each address on your local network so devices show up. Network watch (every 10 minutes after your first scan, unless you turn it off) only reads the devices your computer has already seen. Both ask your router whether UPnP is on, check whether it accepts Telnet or unencrypted web logins, and look up device names using your normal DNS settings (usually answered by your router). Nothing is sent to SentySurf.
  • Family mode (only if you set it up). See below.
  • SIEM forwarding (off unless you turn it on). Alerts and scan results go only to the destinations you set (Windows Event Log, a local file, your syslog server or your HTTPS collector). Nothing goes to SentySurf.
  • Safety check. Links are rated on your computer and are never opened, looked up or sent. Files are checked on your computer: on Windows by Microsoft Defender when it's your active antivirus, on a Mac by Gatekeeper. Defender and Gatekeeper may consult Microsoft or Apple under their own settings. “Look up on VirusTotal” opens your browser with the file's SHA-256 fingerprint, never the file, and only when you click it.
  • Links you click (Help, Store page, VirusTotal and similar) open in your browser; those sites' own privacy policies apply.

Nothing else is sent anywhere. Connections use HTTPS with certificate checking, except the local network checks above and syslog over UDP or TCP if you choose those.

Family mode

About every 20 minutes, and right away when protection turns on or off or a family member presses Refresh, each enrolled computer shares a short summary: device name, SentySurf version, operating system and version, maker and model, processor type, memory, disk size and free space, when it was last started, score, issue counts, the number of apps with updates, whether protection is on or paused, and the titles of high and medium alerts from the last 14 days that haven't been marked as reviewed.

The summary is encrypted on the computer with a key made from your family code (Fernet: AES-128 with HMAC-SHA256; key derived with PBKDF2, 200,000 rounds) before it's sent, on a channel name that's also derived from the code. Nobody without the code can read it, including us.

It goes to both SentySurf's relay at relay.sentysurf.com (run on Cloudflare) and the public ntfy.sh service, so the family keeps working if one is down. The relays see only encrypted data, its size and time, and, like any server, your IP address; SentySurf doesn't log or use it. relay.sentysurf.com deletes messages after 12 hours. ntfy.sh keeps them for a limited time under its own policy (about 12 hours at the time of writing). If you also set a shared folder, the same encrypted summary is written there every 5 minutes. No files, browsing, camera or microphone activity, serial numbers, account names or addresses are included.

Changes SentySurf makes to your computer

SentySurf only changes, removes or ends something when you click a button that clearly says so, such as Fix it, Update all, Remove or End it, or when you turn on live protection, which registers SentySurf to start when you sign in. There are two exceptions:

  • Automatic updates (on by default, can be turned off): verified new versions of SentySurf are installed.
  • Ransomware shield (on by default, can be turned off): two small hidden tripwire documents are placed in each of your main folders (Documents, Desktop, Pictures, Videos, Music, Downloads and any you add). If it sees files being encrypted, it pauses the app most likely doing it, which you can resume or end in one click. Tripwires never leave your computer. If those folders are synced (for example by OneDrive or iCloud), the tripwires are synced with them like any other file. Turning the shield off removes them, and so does uninstalling on Windows; on a Mac or Linux, turn the shield off before removing the app.

Browser check

The browser check runs entirely in your browser. It reads details any website can read (browser version, language, screen size and so on) and shows them to you; it doesn't send them anywhere, store them or set cookies. The only request it makes is for a small file listing current browser versions.

This website

sentysurf.com sets no cookies and uses no analytics, advertising or tracking scripts; fonts and images are served from the site itself. The site is delivered through Cloudflare, which processes each request (including your IP address) to deliver and protect the site under its own privacy policy. Download buttons take you to the release files on GitHub, where GitHub's privacy policy applies. If you email support, we use your message only to answer you.
