The coverage antivirus leaves out.

Sixteen checks across four jobs. All of them run on your computer, and most run all the time.

Detect

  • Remote-access sessions

    AnyDesk, TeamViewer or Quick Assist starting up or accepting a connection. Built for the tech-support scam.

  • Baseline drift

    New startup entries, services, scheduled tasks, listening ports and changes to sensitive files.

  • Traffic redirection

    Altered DNS, proxy settings and homepage overrides, the usual signs of a hijack.

  • Clipboard hijacking

    A copied crypto wallet address swapped for an attacker's, flagged within a second.

Harden

  • Posture score

    Firewall, antivirus, disk encryption, UAC, Remote Desktop, automatic sign-in and backups, rechecked daily.

  • Vulnerable software

    Missing security updates, unsupported Windows, macOS and Ubuntu releases, and exploitable app versions.

  • Ransomware shield

    Hidden tripwire files catch encryption as it starts, and the app doing it is paused.

  • Home network

    Every device on your Wi-Fi, new arrivals flagged, router checked for UPnP and Telnet.

Inspect

  • Links before you click

    Look-alike domains, swapped letters, hidden redirects and risky downloads.

  • Files before you open

    Disguised extensions, macros and broken signatures, plus your system's own malware scan.

  • Browser extensions

    A 0–100 risk score per extension, permissions in plain language, policy-locked installs removed.

  • Camera & microphone

    Each app that turns them on, straight from the system's own records.

Connect

  • SIEM forwarding

    Alerts and scan results to Splunk, Sentinel, Elastic, QRadar or Wazuh over Event Log, syslog or HTTPS.

  • Family console

    Every computer you look after on one screen, end-to-end encrypted with a family code.

  • Mac and Linux

    FileVault, Gatekeeper and SIP on Mac; firewall, disk encryption, SSH and automatic updates on Linux.

  • Browser check

    A free web check of any browser's version, WebRTC exposure and fingerprint. Try it

Remote access

Stops the tech-support scam at the first connection.

Scammers phone, pretend to be Microsoft or your bank, and talk you into installing a remote-control app. SentySurf flags the app the moment it starts and raises a high-risk alert as soon as someone connects, on your computer or any computer in your family.

Network

Know every device on your Wi-Fi.

One scan lists the phones, TVs and laptops on your network. After that, SentySurf notices newcomers by itself and checks your router for the settings botnets look for.

  • Name devices and mark the ones you know
  • An alert when an unknown device joins
  • UPnP and Telnet exposure on the router

Safety check

A second opinion before you click.

Paste a link from a text or email and SentySurf tells you what's wrong with it, without ever opening it. Pick a download and it checks who signed it, where it came from and what's inside.

  • paypa1.com and rnicrosoft.com caught as look-alikes
  • invoice.pdf.exe caught as a disguised program
  • Breached-password check that never sends your password

Family

Look after the computers you're responsible for.

Put SentySurf on a parent's laptop or a kid's desktop and enter your family code. Each one reports its status to you, and you hear about it the moment one needs help.

  • No accounts or sign-ups
  • End-to-end encrypted with a key made from the code
  • Security status only, never files or browsing
  • Hardware inventory for every computer, exportable to CSV

What SentySurf won't do.

  • Remove malware. That's your antivirus's job. SentySurf tells you something's wrong and points you to the fix.
  • Read your files, email or browsing. It looks at settings and system state, never at what you've written or where you've been.
  • Let you spy on your family. Family mode shares a security summary. No screens, messages or history.
  • Promise you're safe. A high score means fewer weak spots. It doesn't prove nothing's wrong.
  • Ask for an account, a card or your email. There's nothing to sign up for, now or later.
  • Come signed, yet. Windows and macOS may warn you the first time you open it while code signing is set up. What to do

Install it, note one code, fix what's red.

There's nothing to configure. Updates install themselves once their checksum checks out.

  1. Install

    Run the installer. Live protection starts for every account on the computer.

  2. Write down your fingerprint

    SentySurf takes a snapshot of the system and shows a 16-character code. If it ever changes and you didn't do it, someone tampered with SentySurf's records.

  3. Fix, then add family

    Work through anything red or amber, then add the other computers you look after.
